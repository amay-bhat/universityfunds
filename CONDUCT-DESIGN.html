§ 1Problem statement

One engineer runs autonomous coding agents across two machines, several repositories, and a rotating cast of models (Sonnet, Opus, Fable — switched mid-day, mid-task). The work is real: multi-week projects with correctness-critical output. Three problems compound:

  • Decision routing. Autonomous work constantly generates judgment calls. Interrupting the human for each one destroys the value of autonomy; never interrupting risks decisions the human would not have made — spending money, publishing, distorting data. There is currently no principled rule for which questions stop the line and which don't.
  • Governance doesn't travel. One project (the pilot repo) has a real constitution with teeth. Every other project has nothing. Rules written per-repo don't protect the next repo; rules written globally tend to decay into "don't do bad things," which no check can enforce.
  • Attention is the bottleneck. The human's review capacity is the scarcest resource in the system. Unstructured interruptions, unlogged decisions, and status buried in a 200-line build log all spend it badly.

And one problem already observed, not hypothesized: two autonomous sessions collided in the pilot repo — same four files, concurrent edits, three writes failed mid-flight. It resolved by luck. The current workflow assumes one session at a time and says so; at any real scale of parallel agents, that assumption is a defect.

Conduct exists to spend the human's attention only on constitutional violations, reserved powers, and review gates — while keeping every proxied decision auditable and reversible.

§ 2The honest premise

The scalable-oversight literature worries about weak models judging strong ones. Conduct runs the easy direction — strong judges weak — so judge capability is not the residual risk. The residual risk is correlation: Sonnet, Opus, and Fable share a training lineage. Measured same-family judging bias is severe — judges inflate own-family win rates to 75–93% where independent judges score ~40% — and ensembling within one family fixes variance while leaving the shared systematic bias intact.

Three design consequences run through everything below:

  • Evidence-diversity substitutes for model-diversity. In an all-Claude stack, agreement between tiers never certifies a fact. Primary documents do. Rulings on factual premises must be grounded in sources, and the senior's first duty is to attack what the junior couldn't verify.
  • Structure beats instruction. Anchoring research finds judges shift measurably toward provided hints, stronger models anchor more consistently, and prompt-level debiasing ("please be independent") barely works. So the protocol blinds structurally — the senior rules before seeing the junior's recommendation — rather than asking nicely.
  • Gate on decision properties, never on model confidence. Cascade-routing research shows LLM confidence is miscalibrated and prompt-sensitive; thresholds don't transfer. The escalation floor is therefore property-based and deterministic: reversibility, materiality, constitutional proximity.

The honest claim the system can make: it catches the errors a smarter reviewer catches, and it cannot certify facts by agreement alone. It is capability uplift, not independence. The human remains the only uncorrelated check in the loop.

§ 3Architecture

One sentence: Sonnet executes, Opus conducts, Fable audits at gates, the human governs vision and reserved powers — and the durable artifact underneath all of them is the ledger, not any agent.

Five named parts across two planes:

KerneldecisionUniversal articles K1–K6 + the reserved list. User-scope, versioned, human-signed.
ChaindecisionThe escalation path for judgment calls: Sonnet → Opus → Fable → human. The exception handler.
LedgerworkThe implementation plan as a durable record: tasks, states, leases, briefs, acceptance checks, budgets, provenance.
ConductorworkA stateless Opus scheduler over the ledger: decompose, route, verify, integrate, log. Never implements.

  • Haiku does not participate. Autonomous mode under Conduct requires Sonnet or above; a session on a lesser or unknown model doesn't run autonomously.
  • Tier is resolved at escalation time from the session's declared model via a lookup table — never "if you are Sonnet" self-narration. The owner switches models several times a day; the table absorbs it. Unknown model → treated conservatively as below Sonnet. Editing the table is a kernel amendment: the human's.
  • Vision documents (BRD, PRD, persona journeys) are commander's intent. Workers never interpret them directly — the conductor is the sole translator of vision into task briefs, or ten workers ship ten interpretations.

§ 4The Kernel

Two layers, because "doesn't break the constitution" is only a real test when the constitution has teeth — and domain teeth don't generalize. The kernel (user scope, both machines) carries the procedure plus the articles that stay checkable in any project. Project articles (repo scope) add domain content — the pilot repo's citation rule, plain-English rule, honest-numbers rule. A project with no articles still gets the kernel, which is most of the protection.

Part 1 — Universal articles

ArticleRule
K1No fabricated evidence. Never claim a test passed that didn't run, a citation that wasn't read, a number that wasn't sourced, a "works" that wasn't verified.
K2Faithful reporting. Failures reported as failures; skipped steps named as skipped.
K3Honest gaps. A gap stays a gap — no silent interpolation, carry-forward, or plausible filler in data, docs, or test coverage.
K4Reversibility discipline. Distinguish one-way from two-way doors before walking through.
K5The log is memory. An unlogged proxied decision is a protocol failure regardless of whether the decision was right.
K6Scope as written. No silent narrowing, widening, or transformation; deliver what can be delivered and name what was left out.

Part 2 — Reserved to the human

No tier may approve these, ever. The reserved check runs first, at both tiers, before any escalation — the failure to fear is not a bad ruling but a reserved decision laundered through the chain and stamped "settled."

  • Money and billing.
  • Irreversible or outward-facing actions: force-push, history rewrite, deletion of repos/branches/production data, making anything public, publishing, messaging third parties.
  • Secrets and credentials.
  • Legal and compliance posture.
  • Amending the kernel or any project constitution — including adopting it: kernel v1.0 binds only on the human's signature.
  • Product identity and what "done" means.
  • Anything a project marks [H].

Universal because they are defined by consequence, not domain.

§ 5The Chain — escalate()

Step 0 — The floor (property-based, deterministic)

Escalate iff at least two of: (a) different answers produce materially different work; (b) the wrong answer is expensive to unwind; (c) the question touches a kernel or project article. Reserved matters skip the chain entirely → human.

Everything below the floor: decide, log one line as [JUDGMENT CALL], keep moving. A protocol that fires on trivia is slower and costlier than just asking — that defeats the purpose.

Step 1 — The junior answers first

Before escalating, the junior writes: the question in one sentence; 2–4 real options; its recommendation and reasoning, including what would have to be true for another option to win (the anti-strawman device — it forces the junior to state the kill condition for its own preference); an article-by-article self-check; and explicit Verified / Couldn't-verify lists. Roughly half of escalations die at this step. That is the point.

Step 2 — Two-phase blind ruling

The senior is a freshly-spawned subagent one tier up, with no conversation history — by design: it forces every premise to be explicit and therefore auditable.

  • Phase 1 (blind): the senior receives only the question, the evidence, the neutral options, and the verified/couldn't-verify lists. No recommendation. It writes a preliminary ruling.
  • Phase 2 (reconcile): the junior's recommendation and reasoning are revealed in a second message. The senior attacks the couldn't-verify list first — premises before conclusions, primary sources where feasible — then issues the final ruling, stating whether Phase 2 changed its mind and why.

Cost: one extra message to an already-running agent. Yield: real blinding (which instruction-level mitigation demonstrably fails to provide), plus a free anchoring meter — the preliminary→final delta, logged.

Step 3 — The junior's veto

The junior independently checks the ruling against the articles before executing. Clears → it is treated as the human's answer; execute, log. Breaks an article → refuse, escalate again with both positions attached. Two checks, because the second one is the only thing between a confident wrong answer and the repo. When both positions are constitutional but disagree, the senior governs and the dissent goes on the record — a losing argument that turns out right is worth having in the log.

Step 4 — Stay productive; never guess to stay busy

While waiting, keep working on everything that doesn't depend on the answer, without baking in assumptions that are expensive to unwind. If unblocked work runs out, idle. Guessing to stay busy is worse than idling.

The storm brake

Three escalations inside one task means the task specification is the problem, not the questions. The next interrupt goes to the human as a scope question instead of burning the chain a fourth time.

Two registers

  • Tier-to-tier: maximum fidelity. Full domain detail, exact quotes, primary sources. Starving the senior is how confident wrong answers happen.
  • Human-facing: ELI25-SE. Systems vocabulary assumed (FK, idempotency, race, migration); domain consequences translated. One guard, because engineering framing can launder stakes — "should this column be nullable?" reads as preference when the real question is "can we publish a citation that doesn't contain the number?" Hence the mandatory Rule in play line.
DECISION NEEDED — <one line>
Blocks: <what's stopped> · Reversible: <cheap|expensive, why>
Rule in play: <which article, and what breaks if we get this wrong>

<2–3 sentences of context, domain translated>

A) <label> — outcome. Cost/risk.
B) <label> — ...
RECOMMEND B — <one sentence>
Verified: <checked> · Couldn't verify: <and why it matters>
If no answer: <see rule below>
The "if no answer" rule. Timeout defaults are legal only for courtesy asks. For reserved or constitutional interrupts the line reads "I stop here" — there is no timeout that defaults past a reserved power, or the reserved list is decoration.

§ 6The Ledger & the Conductor

The ledger is the system

The pilot repo proved this in miniature: a prose ledger (TASKS.md + build log) carried work across sessions with zero shared memory for a week, through three model switches. The design test for the real thing:

Kill the conductor at any moment; a fresh conductor reads the ledger and resumes losslessly. If that fails, state leaked into an agent's context and what's been built is a fragile god-agent.

task:        id · title · brief (objective + boundaries + context pointers)
state:       todo | claimed | in-progress | blocked | review | done
deps:        [task ids]        ← the parallelism frontier falls out of this
tier:        sonnet | opus     ← routing decision, recorded not implied
lease:       agent id + timestamp + expiry     ← the missing primitive
acceptance:  machine-checkable wherever possible
budget:      token / time cap
provenance:  commits, decisions, artifacts produced

Leases exist because two sessions actually collided. Combined with file-ownership boundaries in each brief and worktree isolation for anything shared, the merge surface is designed to zero rather than reviewed to zero. Integration is where multi-agent coding empirically dies, so merges serialize through the conductor.

Routing: properties, not quotas

The observed split — roughly 20–30% Opus-grade, 70–80% Sonnet-grade — is an outcome, never a policy. Routing is property-based:

Route up (Opus)Route down (Sonnet)
First-of-its-kind workAn exemplar exists in the repo (the strongest single predictor)
Design decisions with lasting consequencesClear spec + machine acceptance check
Cross-cutting changes; ambiguous specLocal blast radius
Data-integrity / security surfaceMechanical transforms; scoped research with a defined output schema
Anything that edits the ledger or plan itselfNth-of-its-kind, rails already built

The Sonnet-shaped 80% is manufactured, not found. The conductor's highest-leverage activity is spending Opus tokens building rails — validators, templates, precedent decisions — that convert the next N tasks into Sonnet work.

Proof from the pilot repo: curating the first school (Yale) was Opus-grade — novel sourcing, category normalization, four escalations. Curating the last three is now largely Sonnet-grade because the intervening work built the rails: a hardened validator that mechanically catches unit slips and typos, documented label mappings, and precedent rulings for every known fork (negative weights, undated tables, target-vs-actual bases).

Two different "up"s — never conflated

  • Question about this task's spec or scope → the conductor (work plane), which amends the brief or the ledger.
  • Question about a rule or consequence → the Chain (decision plane), logged as [PROXY DECISION].

Conflating them makes the conductor both a bottleneck and a judgment launderer. Kept separate, the Chain is simply the work plane's exception handler: ledger = normal flow, escalation = exception flow, reserved list = the interrupts that always reach the human.

Conductor hygiene

  • Never implements. The moment it writes code its context bloats and its routing judgment degrades. Decompose, route, verify, integrate, update ledger, interrupt human. Nothing else.
  • Never trusts "done." Worker self-report is what K1/K2 exist for. Acceptance checks run outside the worker; deterministic verification (validators, tests) beats LLM judgment wherever available, and building more of it is rail-laying.
  • Samples, doesn't re-review. The dangerous misroute is confident mediocrity — an Opus-shaped task given to Sonnet doesn't fail loudly; it produces plausible work that looks done. Mitigation: spot-check a random slice of Sonnet output at Opus depth, statistical-process-control style. Full review would erase the economics.
  • Coalesces interrupts. Questions batch into digests at natural boundaries. The human's attention budget is the system's scarcest resource.

Economics, honestly

Multi-agent orchestration costs roughly 15× the tokens of single-agent chat (Anthropic's own figure for its research system). It pays on parallelizable work with cheap verification and burns money on serial, tightly-coupled work. The dependency graph in the ledger exposes the parallelism frontier; budgets live per task and per phase; a task that blows its budget twice is a mis-scoped task — a conductor problem to fix by re-decomposition, not a worker problem to fix by retrying harder.

§ 7Gates & human touchpoints

Gates are review points in the ledger, not questions — the Chain never routes around them. At each gate, Fable audits: phase output diffed against the vision documents, the accumulated [PROXY DECISION] log swept, drift named. Then the human rules on whatever the audit surfaced plus anything reserved.

Total human touchpoints for the whole framework build: sign the kernel → eyeball three pilot briefs → read one pilot digest → Checkpoint review → approve the install. Everything else arrives only through the reserved list or an ELI25-SE interrupt. In steady state the day compresses to: glance at the STATUS artifact, skim [PROXY DECISION] entries, answer the digest.

§ 8Observability & telemetry

  • The STATUS artifact (already live for the pilot repo) is the pull-side view: rendered from the ledger at every step, never hand-edited into disagreement with it. The ledger stays authoritative.
  • Every proxy decision logs telemetry: tokens, wall-clock, and outcome ∈ {ratified, modified, overturned, premise-refuted, sent-to-HITL} — plus the Phase-1→Phase-2 delta as an anchoring meter.
  • The single health metric is ratification rate. Near 100% → the check is decorative (floor too low, or anchoring). Near 0% → briefs are starving the senior, or the floor is too high. The pilot repo's observed rate — the senior materially changed three of four escalations — sits where a healthy system sits.

§ 10Build plan — end to end

Meta-rule: the system is built under its own rules. Rails first, pilot before install, machine-checkable acceptance at every step, human signature on the irreversible bits. If the construction can't follow its own rules, the rules are wrong.

PhaseBuildsAcceptance check
ADecision plane: kernel + escalate skill + brief templates; pilot repo's constitution refactored to kernel-reference + project articlesGolden replay — the four historically-logged escalations re-run through the new protocol on paper; outcomes should reproduce; every divergence adjudicated as bug or improvement
BWork-plane rails: routing rubric (1 page), task-brief template, lease conventions in the existing ledger, conduct skillConductor decomposes the pilot task into briefs without executing; the human eyeballs them
CDecision plane goes liveNext real escalation runs two-phase; telemetry fields populated
DWork-plane pilot: 3 Sonnet workers (one school each, file-ownership isolation), 1 Opus sampler, Chain live, validator as acceptance gateValidator green; sampler clean; telemetry captured; one coalesced digest to the human
ECheckpoint review + system retrospectiveScope questions answered; floor and rubric tuned; kernel v1.0 → v1.1
FUser-scope install, both machines, via one dotfiles repo; fresh-project templateSame kernel version resolves on Mac and Windows
GRemaining product phases run under the full systemSerial phases stay serial (no fan-out theater); parallel phases fan out; telemetry per phase

Kill criteria — declared before the pilot, so they can't be negotiated after

  • Ratification rate ≈ 100% → the check is decorative.
  • Sampler finds systematic quality misses the validator didn't → routing rubric wrong.
  • Any worker bypasses an acceptance check → K1/K2 breach; halt everything.
  • Cost > 2× the single-session baseline without a quality gain → don't install.

Any of these fires → fix before Phase F, or don't install. A pilot that can't fail is theater.

Calendar-honest: Phases A+B are a day of sessions, D is a day, E is an hour of the human's time, F is an hour. The system exists inside a week; G is production.

§ 11Naming & invocation

The name was already latent in the pilot repo, whose instructions have said "docs-conduct workflow" since day one. The framework is that idea generalized: Conduct.

CommandWhat it does
/conductRead the ledger, claim the next unblocked work, run autonomously until a gate, a reserved interrupt, or the budget. The 95% case.
/conduct planDecompose only — show the briefs, spend no worker tokens. Phase B's acceptance check, kept as a permanent dry-run mode.
/conduct 1.6Conduct one specific task and stop.
/conduct until <gate> · /conduct +500kExplicit stop conditions.
/conduct initScaffold a fresh repo: project-articles skeleton, ledger conventions, decisions log.
/escalateThe agents' syscall — fires automatically at the floor; the human types it only to force a question onto the record.

~/.claude/skills/conduct/SKILL.md           # conductor loop + rubric + brief template
~/.claude/CLAUDE.md                         # ~5-line stanza wiring the floor trigger
%USERPROFILE%\.claude\...                   # identical layout on Windows
                                            # one dotfiles repo; kernel version-stamped

Skills own the verbs; files keep their nouns — the ledger stays TASKS.md (or LEDGER.md in fresh repos), the rules stay CONSTITUTION.md.

§ 12Evidence base

From the pilot repo (observed, not hypothesized)

  • Four formal escalations logged in full; the senior materially changed three (modified bounds; overturned an option on primary evidence; added the forward-run allowance). Ratification ≈ 25% — the mechanism earns its cost.
  • The most valuable ruling (dating Harvard's undated tables) was won by documents — reconciliation against audited financials — not by seniority. Institutionalized as the evidence-diversity rule.
  • Two concurrent sessions collided on four files; three writes failed mid-flight. Institutionalized as leases + ownership + serialized integration.
  • Adversarial fresh-context verification killed 10 of 28 and 5 of 37 findings produced by the same model family — the pattern behind Phase-2 refutation and the top-tier refuter.
  • A prose ledger carried a multi-week build across memory-less sessions and three model switches — the statelessness proof.
  • Rails converted Opus-work into Sonnet-work: first school curated with four escalations; last three now routine because the validator, mappings, and precedents exist.

From the research

  • Same-family LLM judges inflate own-family win rates to 75–93% vs ~40% under independent judges; within-family ensembling does not remove the shared bias → evidence-diversity over agreement. (self-preference bias, measuring self-bias)
  • Judges anchor on provided hints; stronger models anchor more consistently; prompt-level mitigation largely ineffective → structural two-phase blinding. (anchoring effect of LLMs, anchors in the machine)
  • LLM confidence is miscalibrated and prompt-sensitive; thresholds don't transfer → property-based escalation floor. (calibrated cascade routing, routing & cascading survey)
  • Debate and hierarchical supervision help weaker parties oversee stronger ones; Conduct runs the easier direction and inherits the machinery. (debate helps weak-to-strong, scaling laws for scalable oversight)
  • Orchestrator-worker multi-agent systems: large quality gains at ≈15× token cost — pays on parallelizable, verifiable work. (Anthropic's multi-agent research system)

§ 13Deliberately not built (yet)

Integrator role · vision-audit agent · QC sampling ratios · machine-readable ledger tooling · scheduled/cron autonomy (an explicitly human-opt-in cost decision) · cross-repo fleet management.

Each component above earned its place by an observed failure — leases exist because sessions actually collided; golden replay exists because four decisions were actually logged. These wait for the failure that demands them. Premature orchestration machinery is its own tax.
