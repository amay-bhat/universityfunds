- **The unit was routed to Opus on the hypothesis that MIT publishes no allocation table at all. That hypothesis is wrong, and the correction is the main finding.** MIT publishes none in its annual report — 20 editions of the *Report of the Treasurer* (FY2006–FY2025) were read end to end and not one contains an allocation percentage table. But MIT has answered **two congressional endowment inquiries**, and both answers print one: the March 3 2008 response to Senators Baucus and Grassley (Table 6C, "2008 Target Allocations for Endowment") and the April 1 2016 response to Senator Hatch and Congressmen Brady and Roskam (Table 1.2, "Endowment Asset Allocation", columns 2015 / 2014 / 2013, headed "Asset Allocation as a Percentage of the Endowment"). Neither document is an annual report and neither would be found by looking where the other four schools keep their data. **Carry forward: for an opaque endowment, congressional-inquiry responses are a primary-source class in their own right** — they are the school's own signed statement, they answer exactly the questions this project asks, and they are published by the school. (Harvard, Stanford and Princeton all answered the same two inquiries.)

MITIMCo's 10-Year Letter (Feb 2017): "Ten years ago, our process started with a top-down assessment of asset class risk and return characteristics and the establishment of asset class target allocations... In 2011, we formalized our shift in thinking and adopted a new manager-centric capital allocation framework." MIT's last quantified allocation statement of any kind is in the FY2011 Report of the Treasurer; the sentence is simply gone from FY2012 onward.

- **Trap avoided, worth naming: MIT publishes a "Net Endowment Growth (%)" column right next to its return column, and it is not a return.** The 2008 Senate response (Table 6A) and 2016 response (Table 3.1) both print growth and return side by side, and MIT defines growth as including gifts and spending. FY2000 reads 51.0% growth against a 55.6% return; FY2009 reads −20.8% growth against a −17.1% return. A regex that grabbed the wrong column would produce plausible, cited, wrong numbers that no sum rule could catch.
- **Second trap: MIT's audited Note B "Investments" table covers all of MIT's investments, not the endowment.** At June 30 2025 it totals $35,790.3M against a $27,366.2M endowment; MIT's 2016 response itemises the difference (Pool C, bond proceeds, life income funds). So for MIT the rejected NAV derivation is not merely a different *basis* — it is a different *entity*.
